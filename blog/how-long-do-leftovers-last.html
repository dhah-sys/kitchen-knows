How long do leftovers last in the fridge?

By Dhiraj Pandey, maker of Tum Tum · November 16, 2026 · 5 min read

Most cooked leftovers keep 3 to 4 days in the fridge, according to the USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service (FSIS). That only holds if they went into the fridge within 2 hours of cooking and your fridge is at 40°F (4°C) or below. If you will not eat them in time, freeze them.

Below are the rules that keep leftovers safe, from the moment the food comes off the stove to the moment you reheat it.

The short answer: 3 to 4 days

FSIS says leftovers can be kept in the refrigerator for 3 to 4 days, or frozen for 3 to 4 months. The FoodSafety.gov cold storage chart gives the same fridge time for common leftovers:

LeftoverFridge, 40°F (4°C) or belowFreezer, 0°F (-18°C), for best quality
Cooked meat or poultry3 to 4 days2 to 6 months
Soups and stews3 to 4 days2 to 3 months
Pizza3 to 4 days1 to 2 months
Chicken nuggets or patties3 to 4 days1 to 3 months
Casseroles with eggs, after baking3 to 4 days2 to 3 months
Egg, chicken, tuna, ham or macaroni salad3 to 4 daysDoes not freeze well
Hard-cooked eggs1 weekDo not freeze

The freezer times are about quality, not safety. FoodSafety.gov says food kept frozen at 0°F (-18°C) or below can be kept indefinitely. After a few months, frozen leftovers just lose moisture and flavor.

Not sure about something that is not on this list? FoodSafety.gov points to the USDA's FoodKeeper, which covers storage times for more than 650 foods and drinks.

The 2-hour rule

The fridge clock only works if the food got there in time. Bacteria grow fast between 40°F and 140°F, which FSIS calls the "danger zone."

  • Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or of taking them off a warmer.
  • If it is hotter than 90°F, such as at a summer picnic, the limit is 1 hour.
  • Throw away perishable food that has sat at room temperature longer than that.

The FDA says the same rule covers take-out food and restaurant "doggie bags." If the bag rode around in a warm car all afternoon, it does not get a fresh 3 to 4 days.

How to cool leftovers fast

A big pot of soup takes a long time to cool in the middle, which gives bacteria time to grow. FSIS recommends:

  • Divide large amounts into shallow containers so they cool quickly.
  • Cut big items, like a roast or ham, into smaller pieces. Slice turkey breast; legs and wings can stay whole.
  • Put hot food straight into the fridge. You do not need to let it cool on the counter first. You can also chill it fast in an ice or cold water bath before refrigerating.

Check your fridge and freezer temperature

The 3 to 4 day rule assumes a cold fridge. The FDA says to keep the refrigerator at or below 40°F (4°C) and the freezer at 0°F (-18°C). Many fridges do not show a real temperature, so the FDA suggests an inexpensive appliance thermometer in the front of each, checked at least once a week.

Also, do not pack the fridge so full that air cannot move. Cold air needs room to circulate.

Store leftovers the right way

Cover and seal

FSIS says to cover leftovers, wrap them airtight, or seal them in containers. This keeps bacteria out, keeps moisture in, and stops food from picking up smells from the rest of the fridge.

Label with a date

Write what it is and the date you cooked it on a piece of tape or a sticky label. "Chili, 11/16" takes five seconds and ends the "how old is this?" guessing game. The EPA also suggests clear, labeled, dated containers, so you can see what you have.

Use one spot for leftovers

Keep them on the same shelf at eye level. When leftovers are scattered, they get pushed to the back and forgotten past day 4. Our guide on how to organize your fridge has more on this.

Freezing leftovers

Freezing is the easy way to stretch leftovers past 3 to 4 days. Freeze them early, while they are still fresh, not on day 4. Freezing does not kill most bacteria, the FDA explains. It stops them from growing. That is why it pays to freeze food while it is fresh.

  • Use airtight containers or freezer bags, and press out the air.
  • Freeze in meal-sized portions, so you only thaw what you need.
  • Label with the name and the date.

Freezer burn, those grayish-brown dry spots, is a quality problem, not a safety one, according to the FDA. It happens when food is not wrapped airtight, so good wrapping prevents most of it.

How to thaw frozen leftovers

FSIS lists three safe ways:

  1. In the fridge. Slowest, but the food stays safe the whole time. Eat within 3 to 4 days, or refreeze.
  2. In cold water. Faster. Keep the food in a leak-proof bag. Cook it before refreezing.
  3. In the microwave. Fastest. Keep heating until it reaches 165°F.

You can also reheat soups, stews and casseroles straight from frozen. It just takes longer.

Reheating leftovers safely

FSIS says to reheat leftovers until they reach 165°F (74°C), measured with a food thermometer.

  • Bring sauces, soups and gravies to a rolling boil.
  • Cover food while reheating. It holds moisture and helps it heat all the way through.
  • In the microwave, cover, stir or turn the food, and add a little liquid if needed. Microwaves leave cold spots, so check the temperature in several places after a short rest.

Can you refreeze leftovers?

Yes, in two cases, per FSIS. Leftovers that were frozen, then reheated to 165°F, can be refrozen. And if you thawed a big container in the fridge and only need part of it, you can refreeze the rest without reheating it.

When in doubt, throw it out

The FDA puts it plainly: if you are not sure, or if the food looks questionable, throw it out. Two points are worth knowing:

  • Smell and looks are not a safety test. The FDA notes that food can make you sick even when it does not look, smell or taste spoiled. The bacteria that cause illness are different from the ones that make food "go bad."
  • Mold means it goes. The FDA says the safest practice is to discard moldy food.

So do not sniff-test a day 6 container of chicken. Go by the date on the label.

Make leftovers easier to use up

The best way to avoid the 3 to 4 day deadline is to plan for leftovers. Cook a double batch on purpose and put it on the plan for two days later, or freeze half right away. More ideas are in how to stop wasting food at home.
